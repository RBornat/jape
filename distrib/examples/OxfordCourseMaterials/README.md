# Introduction to Formal Proof

The materials here were presented as part of a short (12 lecture)
course on formal proof I gave between 2011 and 2021 to *first-year
Computer Science undergraduates* at the start of their third term
of study in Oxford.


I was motivated by the idea that a good deal of the substance of CS
can be (and is) presented in the form of inference systems whose
judgements are *about* things (values, types, programs, ...) and that the
notions of completeness and consistency of such inference systems
can be introduced early when the things are boolean values,
and the inference systems are logics. The fact that (at Oxford)
our first year undergraduates are (in principle) adept at
using functional languages and inductive data types liberated me to
represent formulae and proofs using inductive data types, thereby avoiding
having to spend any time on proofs (etc) represented as texts.

Mathematics & Computer Science, and Computer Science & Philosophy
students were not expected to attend the course, on which *they*
were not examined. And some of those who actually attended
told me that their tutors had expected them *not to attend* the course.

The course was supported by **Jape**, and  `examples/IFPFRESH`
was made available to students who thought they might benefit
by using it.

As I should have expected, some of my more theoretically oriented
colleagues -- all refugee logicians -- thought the course insufficiently
conventional. *Tant-pis!*

#### Bernard Sufrin, Department of Computer Science, Oxford, 2026.
